**candidate.template.title**

> Marks of their own

**candidate.template.summary**

> A blank page, one crayon and no picture to copy.

**candidate.template.instructions**

> Offer the prepared paper and crayon without drawing a model. Your child can make marks, watch or leave it for another time. If they begin, let them choose where the marks go. Stay available; quiet company is enough. If they invite conversation, describe something you can actually see or listen to their explanation without naming a feeling or correcting the picture. The child can stop or turn away. When finished, the adult puts the materials away and supports ordinary handwashing.

**candidate.template.conversation\_prompt**

> Use a short description of a visible mark if conversation is welcome. If your child tells you what it is, use their meaning; there is no need to ask them to explain.

**candidate.template.look\_for**

> If you want to, notice whether they return to a mark, add another or choose to stop. A note is optional and is not a measure of skill or creativity.

**candidate.template.why\_it\_matters**

> This offers space to make something without a predetermined result. NAEYC describes this kind of choice-led process art in preschool practice. That is a practice rationale, not evidence that this particular activity improves development.

**candidate.template.safety\_note**

> Use only the listed materials with readable original product information and an age/use label that includes this child. No safety label makes a crayon edible or prevents breakage. Keep materials out of mouths, food and drinks away from this workspace, and younger children away from these supplies. Stay attentive throughout. Stop and remove the materials if mouthing, breakage, an unusual reaction or unsafe use occurs. Do not continue through discomfort or override individual professional guidance.

**candidate.template.parent\_preparation**

> Check the labels and condition before offering one intact solid wax crayon and plain paper. Reject supplies with unknown origin, missing labels, hazard warnings, detachable parts or broken pieces. Use an already accessible, stable, uncluttered drawing surface, away from an adult art studio or stored chemicals. Keep extra supplies out of reach. The two-minute setup and five-minute activity estimates are planning allowances, not a required duration.

**candidate.template.adult\_role**

> Prepare the space, stay available and follow the child's choices. Do not guide their hand, correct their grip or add a task to make the picture more advanced. Help with setup and putting materials away; cleanup is not a test or a condition of approval.

**candidate.template.child\_choices**

> They may make marks or not, leave the page unfinished, tell you what it means, say nothing, ask for help or stop.

**candidate.template.make\_easier**

> Offer the same materials without a question or a model. If using them is uncomfortable or requires a new physical adaptation, omit this activity until an appropriate adaptation has been independently reviewed.

**candidate.template.extend\_activity**

> If they choose to return to their own marks during the same supervised opportunity, leave space for another mark with the same materials. Add no new tools, technique, target or demand to continue.

**candidate.template.stop\_signals**

> Stop on refusal, turning away, discomfort, mouthing, broken crayon pieces, unsafe handling or a need for care. Stop if the adult cannot remain attentive. A changed setting or material is not an approved variation.

**candidate.template.avoid\_prompt**

> No picture to copy, colour quiz, tidy-picture standard, comparison with another child or interpretation of their marks as a mood, ability or diagnosis.
